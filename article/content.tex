\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Fachechi, Agliari and Barra \supercite{fachechi2019dreaming} (Neural
Networks 112, 2019, 24--40; arXiv:1810.12217) revisit the ``unlearning''
idea of Hopfield, Feinstein and Palmer \supercite{hopfield1983unlearning}
and derive a closed-form ``dreaming'' kernel for the Hopfield model.
Starting from the Hebbian coupling matrix
\(J = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mu=1}^{P}\xi^\mu\xi^{\mu\mathsf T}\) built from
\(P = \alpha N\) random \(\pm1\) patterns, a sleep phase of duration
\(t\) produces

\[J(t) = J\,(1+t)\,(\mathbb 1 + tJ)^{-1},\]

which equals \(J\) at \(t = 0\) and tends to the projection
(pseudo-inverse) rule of Kanter and Sompolinsky
\supercite{kanter1987projector} as \(t \to \infty\). The article's
statistical-mechanics analysis predicts that the critical storage load
\(\alpha_c(t)\) rises from the Amit--Gutfreund--Sompolinsky value
\supercite{amit1985storing} \(\approx 0.138\) at \(t = 0\) towards the
theoretical bound \(\alpha = 1\), that spurious (mixture) attractors are
removed, that pure patterns are reinforced, and that the gain is robust
to thermal noise. The original Monte Carlo checks (Section 2.5 of the
published article) use, for the magnetisation curves, single-spin-flip
Glauber dynamics measured after equilibration on networks of
\(N = 5\,000\) neurons averaged over \(M = 10\) pattern realisations
(their Fig. 7, loads \(P/N \in \{0.02, 0.08, 0.32\}\),
\(t \in \{0, 0.1, 1, 10,
100, 1000\}\)) and a finite-size comparison at \(N \in \{500, 1\,000,
5\,000\}\) (their Fig. 8). The zero-noise internal-field and basin
analyses (their Figs. 9--10) use the \emph{parallel} sign update of
their Eq. (2.17) at \(N = 1\,000\), \(P = 50\). For the fields (Fig. 9)
the network starts from random configurations, 20 evolutions for each of
20 pattern realisations; for the basins (Fig. 10) it starts from the
first stored pattern with each bit flipped independently with
probability \(p\), success being declared when that pattern carries the
largest Mattis overlap, 200 evolutions for each of 200 realisations. The
replica-symmetric capacity rises from \(\alpha_c(0) \simeq 0.138\) to
\(\alpha_c \simeq 1.07\) as \(t \to \infty\), the excess over Gardner's
bound being attributed to replica-symmetry breaking. No capacity curve
is measured by Monte Carlo in the original: the simulations verify
magnetisations, fields and basins against the theory.

We independently implement the published kernel and \emph{partially}
replicate its qualitative claims at finite size (\(N \le 1\,000\)) with
a new, noisy-cue capacity estimator and new random seeds: we test the
retrieval gain, the removal of spurious final states, the robustness to
thermal noise and the projector limit, and we do not reproduce the
replica-symmetric capacity curve nor the original Monte Carlo figures.
Each of the three studies was pre-registered and frozen by an annotated
git tag before its own main run; the later registrations were informed
by the preceding studies (Section ``Pre-registration''). The replication
was carried out inside the Hypneum Lab research programme (issue
hypneum-lab/nerve-wml\#68), where the dreaming kernel serves as a
reference operation; the replication stands on its own and makes no
claim about that programme.

Beyond replicating, we add one control absent from the original work: a
uniform multiplicative rescaling (``downscale'') of the Hebbian
couplings, matched to the dreamed kernel on the Frobenius norm. This
control answers a question the original article does not ask --- whether
the benefit of dreaming could be reproduced by a global reduction of
synaptic strength, as in homeostatic-downscaling accounts of sleep
\supercite{tononi2014shy} --- and it provides a calibrated null for the
capacity ratio. Finally, we test the kernel on correlated patterns,
which the original article treats only for independent patterns.

\section{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{Original model}\label{original-model}

Binary units \(s_i \in \{\pm1\}\), \(i = 1 \dots N\); patterns
\(\xi^\mu \in \{\pm1\}^N\) i.i.d. uniform; Hebbian kernel as above;
dreamed kernel \(J(t)\). In pattern space,
\(J_{ij}(t) = \frac1N\sum_{\mu\nu}\xi_i^\mu
[(1+t)(\mathbb 1 + tC)^{-1}]_{\mu\nu}\xi_j^\nu\) with
\(C_{\mu\nu} = \frac1N\xi^\mu\!\cdot\xi^\nu\). The eigenvalues
\(\lambda \ge 0\) of \(J\) map to \(\lambda(1+t)/(1+t\lambda)\).
Retrieval is assessed by the overlap \(m = \frac1N\sum_i s_i\xi_i^\mu\)
of the final state with a stored pattern.

\subsection{Implementation}\label{implementation}

Our code (S1: \texttt{run.py}, \texttt{confirmatory.py},
\texttt{correlated.py}; 713 + 384 + 237 lines, NumPy only, no original
code reused) computes \(J(t)\) by spectral decomposition of the
symmetric \(J\) (\texttt{numpy.linalg.} \texttt{eigh}, negative
numerical eigenvalues clipped to 0). Twenty test functions (23 executed
cases) check that \(J(0) = J\), that the spectral form equals the
pattern-space closed form on 2-pattern and many-pattern cases, that
\(t = 10^{8}\) approximates the projector within the tested tolerance,
that the matched shrink is exactly inert at zero temperature (S2), and
cover the confirmatory and correlated runners.

The diagonal \(J_{ii}\) is set to 0 in every arm before the dynamics.
The dynamics are asynchronous with one fresh random permutation of the
\(N\) units per sweep, shared by the probes of a cell. At \(T = 0\),
\(s_i \leftarrow \operatorname{sign}(h_i)\) with ties (\(|h_i| \le
10^{-12} N \max|J|\)) resolved by keeping the current spin --- a
tolerance chosen so that the rule is exactly invariant under
\(J \to cJ\); the run stops at the first sweep without a flip, cap 100
sweeps (non-convergence counted as not retrieved; none occurred in any
run). At \(T = 0.2\), Glauber updates with
\(P(s_i = +1) = 1/(1+e^{-2\beta h_i})\), \(\beta = 5\), 40 sweeps,
overlap averaged over the last 10.

Cue: a stored pattern with exactly \(\operatorname{round}(0.10\,N)\)
bits flipped (overlap 0.80 at \(N = 1\,000\); 0.8125, 0.797, 0.797 at
\(N = 64\), 128, 256 because of rounding). Probes: the first
\(\min(P, 32)\) stored patterns, i.e.~10 and 20 probes at the loads 0.01
and 0.02 of the correlated run. The pilot also includes a descriptive
\(T = 0\) condition with \(f = 0\) (unperturbed stored-pattern cues),
absent from the two \(N = 1\,000\) studies. Final states are classified
as \texttt{retrieved} (\(m \ge 0.9\); \(0.8\) at \(T = 0.2\)),
\texttt{other\_stored} or \texttt{spurious}. Capacity estimator per
(arm, seed): \(\hat\alpha_c\) is the linearly interpolated 0.5 crossing
of the retrieval fraction \(r(\alpha)\) immediately after the
\emph{last} grid load with \(r \ge 0.5\), on the grid
\(\alpha \in \{0.04,\allowbreak 0.06,\allowbreak \dots,\allowbreak 0.20,\allowbreak 0.25,\allowbreak \dots,\allowbreak 0.95\}\)
(24 values; 0.01 and 0.02 added for the correlated run). If the largest
load still has \(r \ge 0.5\) the estimate is right-censored at 0.95; if
no load reaches 0.5 the code records the flag \texttt{below\_grid} and a
zero sentinel, which marks censoring, not a measured zero capacity.
Patterns are nested in \(\alpha\) and the same patterns, cues and update
orders are used for every arm of a cell (paired design). Statistics are
paired per-seed ratios and differences, mean over 10 seeds with a 95 \%
percentile bootstrap CI (10 000 seed-level resamples, bootstrap seed
20261025). Every random draw comes from
\texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng(}
\texttt{{[}20261025,\ seed,\ N,\ stream{]})}.

\subsection{Matched uniform-shrink control
(added)}\label{matched-uniform-shrink-control-added}

For each \(t > 0\), \(J_c = c(t)\,J\) with
\(c(t) = \|\mathrm{offdiag}\,J(t)\|_F / \|\mathrm{offdiag}\,J\|_F\),
i.e. equal global synaptic budget; \(c\) depends on \(t\), \(\alpha\),
\(N\) and the seed and the correlation level; across the shrink arms it
ranges from 0.0884 to 0.9985. Two facts were declared before any run so
that the control cannot be misread: at \(T = 0\) the sign dynamics is
invariant under \(c > 0\), so the control must give a ratio of exactly 1
(an implementation check, not an empirical hypothesis); at \(T > 0\),
\(J_c\) at inverse temperature \(\beta\) has the same transition
probabilities as \(J\) at \(c\beta\), i.e.~the control is the Hebbian
network at temperature \(T/c\). This identity does not impose a
deterministic inequality on a finite-time, thresholded,
single-realisation capacity estimate (in the pilot, seed 6 at
\(N = 256\), \(T = 0.2\) gives a shrink ratio of 1.012), so the effect
at \(T = 0.2\) is measured empirically.

\subsection{Correlated patterns
(added)}\label{correlated-patterns-added}

One ancestor \(a \in \{\pm1\}^N\) per (seed, level); each pattern bit
equals \(a_i\) with probability \((1+m)/2\), so the mean pairwise
overlap is \(m^2\). Levels \(m \in \{0, 0.15, 0.30\}\)
(\(q \in \{0, 0.0225, 0.09\}\)), seeds 100--109 (disjoint from the
independent-pattern seeds 0--9).

\subsection{Pre-registration and decision
rules}\label{pre-registration-and-decision-rules}

Three L1 pre-registrations, each frozen by an annotated tag pushed to
GitHub before launch and each archived by Software Heritage (verbatim
copies in \texttt{prereg/} of the submission repository, S3):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Pilot} (tag of 2026-10-25, S3): \(N \in \{64, 128,
  256\}\), \(t \in \{0, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100\}\), three conditions.
\item
  \textbf{Confirmatory} (second tag of 2026-10-25): \(N = 1\,000\),
  \(t \in \{0,\allowbreak 1,\allowbreak 3,\allowbreak 5,\allowbreak 7,\allowbreak 10,\allowbreak 15,\allowbreak 20,\allowbreak 30,\allowbreak 100\}\),
  \(T \in \{0, 0.2\}\).
\item
  \textbf{Correlated} (tag of 2026-10-27): as confirmatory, three
  correlation levels, 26 loads.
\end{itemize}

Binding criteria at \(t = 10\): \textbf{C1} (gain) confirmed if the CI
lower bound of the ratio \(R\) of the dreamed (\(t = 10\)) to the
Hebbian capacity at \(T = 0\) is \(\ge 3\), refuted if the CI upper
bound of the paired difference is \(< 0.1\); \textbf{C2′} (shape) holds
if the median curve \(t \mapsto \hat\alpha_c\) is unimodal within 0.02
with an interior maximum \(t^*\); \textbf{C3′} (control) holds if the CI
upper bound of the ratio \(S\) of the shrink (\(c(10)\)) to the Hebbian
capacity at \(T = 0.2\) is \(< 1.1\), refutes if the lower bound is
\(\ge 2\); the \(T = 0\) ratio must be exactly 1 on every seed,
otherwise the run is void. \textbf{P4} (secondary label): spurious final
states at \(\alpha \in \{0.14, 0.20\}\) reduced by \(\ge 50\) \%.
\textbf{C4} (correlated run): paired difference
\(R(m = 0.30) - R(m = 0)\) with CI excluding 0. Seeds whose Hebbian
\(\hat\alpha_c\) is below the grid have an undefined ratio.

These are the confirmatory and correlated criteria. The pilot
registration used the original C2 (monotonicity of the median curve,
tolerated decrease 0.05 per grid step), C3 at \(T = 0\) only, and P4 at
loads 0.10 and 0.14; the confirmatory amendment replaced C2 by C2′ and
moved C3 to \(T = 0.2\) after the pilot had been observed, and the
correlated registration was written after both independent-pattern
studies. The pilot's median curve decreases from 0.600 to 0.522 between
\(t = 10\) and \(t = 100\) and passed C2 only because no single step
exceeded 0.05.

\subsection{What was changed with respect to the original, and
why}\label{what-was-changed-with-respect-to-the-original-and-why}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Finite \(N \le 1\,000\) and an empirical, noisy-cue, 0.5-crossing
  capacity estimator instead of the replica-theory \(\alpha_c(t)\):
  absolute capacities are therefore not comparable with the published
  curve; the replication tests ratios and qualitative claims (gain,
  spurious removal, projector limit).
\item
  Self-coupling removed (\(J_{ii} = 0\)) in all arms: standard for
  retrieval dynamics; it changes the update fields relative to the
  original rule, whose field sums over \(j = 1 \dots N\). We did not run
  an ablation with the diagonal kept, so its contribution to the finite
  optimum in \(t\) reported below is not isolated.
\item
  Dynamics and success criterion: asynchronous updates at \(T = 0\)
  instead of the parallel sign update of the original zero-noise field
  and basin experiments (their Eq. 2.17); a fixed number of flipped cue
  bits, \(\operatorname{round}(0.10\,N)\), instead of independent flips
  with probability \(p\); an absolute overlap threshold (0.9, or 0.8 at
  \(T = 0.2\)) instead of ``largest Mattis overlap''; at \(T = 0.2\), a
  fixed 40-sweep window without an equilibration diagnostic.
\item
  Matched shrink control and correlated patterns: additions, not
  changes.
\item
  Parameters and decision rules fixed before each study's main run
  (grids, seeds, thresholds); one voided launch of the pilot (BLAS
  thread oversubscription, killed after \textasciitilde150 s before the
  main run; log kept in the upstream repository, D1).
\end{enumerate}

Reading rule for \(m = 0.30\). At \(T = 0\) all ten Hebbian crossings
are bracketed by the loads 0.01 and 0.02 (flags \texttt{ok}; the
registration added these two loads for that purpose), so the registered
ratio 44.38 is defined and its C1 label is confirmed. Its bootstrap
interval describes seed variation conditional on the interpolation rule
and does not include the load-grid discretisation error; reading the
crossings as anywhere inside their bracketing intervals gives a ratio
between about 30 and 65. We therefore do not interpret the magnitude as
a precise model-level capacity ratio. At \(T = 0.2\) five Hebbian
estimates are below the grid; among the five seeds with a positive
Hebbian denominator, two shrink estimates are also below the grid. The
archived code replaces below-grid capacities by zero, so the Hebbian
mean 0.007 {[}0.003, 0.011{]} and the five-seed shrink statistic 0.6
{[}0.2, 1.0{]} (per-seed 1, 0, 1, 1, 0) are outputs of that coding
convention, not censoring-adjusted estimates. The absolute capacities at
\(T = 0\), also interpolated but less sensitive to the small
denominator, are the more robust reading: the dreamed capacity barely
moves with correlation (0.611, 0.616, 0.636) while the Hebbian capacity
collapses (0.160, 0.068, 0.014), so the paired absolute gain grows 0.451
{[}0.445, 0.456{]} → 0.548 {[}0.543, 0.552{]} → 0.621 {[}0.618,
0.625{]}. The tagged code labels C3′ ``holds'' and the level
``confirmed'' in \texttt{summary.json}, computed on the five seeds with
a positive denominator. This article keeps that registered label and
adds, next to it, a post-analysis assessment of inconclusive for the
level (decision of 2026-10-30, commit \texttt{5884927}): a
methodological reservation, not a registered rule. Its ground is
censoring: five denominators and two further numerators are below the
grid and coded as zero, so the available bootstrap does not estimate the
ten-seed control ratio; Hebbian retrieval at \(T = 0.2\) survives only
within the first load interval. The registered labels are kept unchanged
in the raw outputs. The pre-registered below-grid rule was written for
C1 only; a future amendment should extend it to C3′ and the load grid

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Under decision rules fixed before each study's main run, this partial
replication \textbf{supports} the central qualitative claims of
Fachechi, Agliari and Barra within our finite-size protocol: the dreamed
kernel raises the empirical storage capacity of the Hopfield network by
a large factor (3.4--3.9 at \(t = 10\) for independent patterns, \(N\) =
256 to 1 000), leaves 0/320 probes classified as spurious at each of the
loads 0.14 and 0.20 in the confirmatory run at \(T = 0\), against 51/320
and 318/320 under Hebb (which does not establish the absence of spurious
attractors elsewhere in state space), keeps its advantage under thermal
noise, and behaves as the projector rule for large \(t\) (the pilot's
\(f = 0\) capacity estimate is right-censored at \(\alpha =
0.95\) at the tested times \(t \in \{10, 30, 100\}\) for
\(N \in \{64, 128,
256\}\) --- a 50 \%-retrieval criterion on at most 32 probed patterns,
not a test that every stored pattern is a fixed point). The ratios 3.86
and 9.06 are results of this study, not numbers published in 2019. On
correlated patterns the gain grows with correlation at \(m \le 0.15\),
consistent with the projector limit's insensitivity to pattern
correlations.

Two points go beyond, or qualify, the original. First, the added control
shows that the Frobenius-matched uniform rescalings of the Hebbian
couplings tested here do not reproduce the gain: they are inert at
\(T = 0\) by scale invariance and give a mean paired relative capacity
reduction of 2.52 \% at \(T = 0.2\) (seed-bootstrap 95 \% interval
1.80--3.35 \%). The dreaming effect is a spectral change of the
attractor landscape, not a global downscaling at equal synaptic budget.
Second, with the self-coupling removed and a noisy cue, the finite-size
capacity is not monotone in \(t\): it peaks at \(t^* = 7\) and loses
0.088 {[}0.082, 0.092{]} by \(t = 100\). This does not contradict the
mean-field \(\alpha_c(t)\): our noisy-cue threshold differs from the
equilibrium retrieval boundary of the theory. We observe a lower
empirical capacity at \(t = 100\) than at the grid optimum \(t = 7\)
without measuring basin sizes or isolating the cause (self-coupling
removal, cue noise, asynchronous dynamics, finite \(N\)). The original
article does quantify retrieval basins, at \(N = 1\,000\) and
\(\alpha = 0.05\) under parallel zero-noise dynamics, and finds them
stabilised by dreaming; that experiment does not cover our higher-load
protocol.

Recent work situates these observations. Benedetti et al.
\supercite{benedetti2024eigenvector} follow the eigenvectors of the
coupling matrix during unlearning, the spectral reading that
distinguishes dreaming from a rescaling in our control. Serricchio et
al. \supercite{serricchio2025daydreaming} combine reinforcement and
removal in a ``daydreaming'' algorithm and report its effectiveness on
correlated data generated by a random-features model, a correlation
structure different from our single ancestor. Lucibello and Mézard
\supercite{lucibello2024exponential} make precise, for dense associative
memories, the distinction between the capacity of a typical pattern, of
all patterns and the size of the basins --- the distinction that also
separates our noisy-cue estimator from a test of the stability of every
stored pattern.

What this replication cannot settle: absolute capacities (finite-size,
estimator-dependent), temperatures other than 0 and 0.2, correlation
structures other than a single ancestor, and the \(m = 0.30\) level,
where the Hebbian crossings lie inside the first load interval
(0.01--0.02) at \(T = 0\) and below the grid on five seeds at
\(T = 0.2\). A scale sweep (nerve-wml\#90) is open and not included
here.
